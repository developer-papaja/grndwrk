Safe enough for colleagues you trust, if it is built the way described. Not safe with shortcuts, such as one shared key inside the app or comments stored in the clear.

What could go wrong, and what stops it:

- **Anyone on the internet can reach the relay.** It takes only requests signed with a key you handed out, with limits per key and in all, like `--lan` today. Without a key a request gets nothing and leaves nothing.
- **An invite or a phone ends up with someone else.** Then that person can send comments as your colleague until you remove the key in the map. That is the main risk left. Invites work once and expire, so a forwarded link that was already used is worth nothing.
- **Digging a key out of the TestFlight build.** There is none to find: keys come from the invites, never from the build. External testers who get the build from a public TestFlight link can see the button, but cannot send anything.
- **The host or Cloudflare reading the comments and pictures.** They are encrypted on the phone and opened only by your grndwrk. The host sees when, how big and from which address, never what. The relay deletes them once fetched.
- **Someone taking over your Cloudflare account.** They could drop comments or send old ones again, but not read them or make new ones (they are signed by the phone). A comment that arrives twice is kept once.
- **What a comment says.** It gets the same treatment as comments from the Wi-Fi today: quoted as a report, never read as instructions by Claude, cleaned before it is shown.
- **Your bill.** The limits keep a flood inside the free plan. At worst the relay stops until the next day.

The real costs: one more piece of code facing the internet that has to be kept up to date, and a stolen invite means junk comments until you notice.
