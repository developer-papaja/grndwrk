Built, after you said to go ahead, with these answers to the three questions above (say in a remark if you want another way):

1. Who writes it: your agent, by the map's rules, whenever it ships a release (tags, publishes, deploys, hands out a build), and `/release <name>`, which lists what it found and writes only after you say yes. Reading git tags by itself is not built.
2. What can be tied to it: stories, built thoughts and fixed comments, by id, in the release's `shipped:` list.
3. Where you see it: Deliverables has a Releases tab (only when there is a release), newest first, each with its date, where it went, its notes and what shipped, a click opening each. A story's or thought's panel ends in Released in, a row per release, which opens that release. No filter yet.

A release is `releases/r_<id>.md` with `name`, `date`, `where` and `shipped`, and notes below. The map only reads them; `grndwrk check` reads them too, and a team's store syncs them.

1. In a project, run `/release`, with or without the version (`/release 0.4.3`). Without one, your agent proposes a name itself: the newest tag, the version the project declares, or else the day. It lists that and what went out since the release before, and writes it down only after you say yes.
2. Open Deliverables: a Releases tab shows the release with its date, where and notes, and what shipped in it. A click on a story or thought there opens it. [agent: tried in a throwaway map in the browser]
3. Open a story or thought that shipped: its panel ends in Released in, one row per release (a story in two releases shows both), and a click on one opens it under Releases. [agent: tried in the same map]
4. Reading release files: newest first, a broken file shown with why, ids and words cleaned. [automated: test/releases.test.ts]
